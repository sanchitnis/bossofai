Chapter One — The Trajectory

The Machine Trajectory

From a rigid arm welding empty air to a humanoid that re-plans its grip when the cup is wet. Four cognitive stages, eighty years of evolution.

Key Bullets

  • ›Reading vs. Doing: A chatbot can define the word "heavy" mathematically — but it has never felt gravity. Embodied intelligence requires interaction.
  • ›Pre-Programmed (1969→): ASEA's IRB-6 — the first electric industrial robot — could repeat one motion 24/7 but ignored the world.
  • ›Connected (IIoT, 2010s): Sensors stream data to the cloud, but humans still close the decision loop.
  • ›Aware (Computer Vision): Robots detect humans & hazards — they react, but cannot reason.
  • ›Adaptive (Physical AI): Perceive → reason → act in a closed loop with the real world.

Numbers to Remember

  • ▸1969: First electric industrial robot (ASEA IRB-6, 6 kg payload).
  • ▸3.9 million: Industrial robots operating worldwide (IFR, 2023).
  • ▸~1.5 trillion params: NVIDIA Cosmos & Google RT-2 foundation models for robotic control.
  • ▸10⁹+ trials: Sim2Real training can run a billion failed grasps without bending a real motor.

Fun Fact

🤖The word "robot" comes from robota — Czech for "forced labor" — coined in Karel Čapek's 1920 play R.U.R.
PART ONE

Why Reading Isn't Doing

Why reading all the books in the world is not enough to help a machine understand how physical objects fall, bend, or break.

The "Dictionary Problem": Chatbots only define words with other words. An AI knows "heavy" mathematically, but it has never felt gravity or weight.
Bicycle Analogy: A human cannot learn to ride a bicycle simply by reading a book about balancing equations. It requires falling, micro-corrections, and tactile muscle memory.
Unpredictable Real World: A virtual world has no dust, friction variations, or sudden liquid spills. Intelligence must physically interact with space to survive.

The D3 Imperative

Moving past the fear of "robots taking our jobs" by focusing on the work no human being should have to do: Deleterious, Dangerous, and Debilitating tasks.

Durable Human Capital: Transitioning humans from being "mechanical pulleys" to safety supervisors running fleet operations.

The Architecture of Physical AI

Three ideas that turn an AI text-machine into an AI action-machine.

Action Tokens

"Words for moves."
  • ›LLMs predict the next word.
  • ›Physical AI predicts the next motion.
  • ›Joint angles + grip force become discrete tokens, learned like grammar.
  • ›The same transformer architecture � just a different vocabulary.
Real example
Google RT-2 � one model that reads a kitchen and decides where the gripper goes next.

Cross-Embodiment

"One brain, many bodies."
  • ›Same model runs a humanoid, a robot arm, a mobile platform.
  • ›Skills transfer across hardware shapes.
  • ›A robot in Tokyo learns friction; a robot in Munich uses it.
  • ›One upgrade lifts the entire global fleet.
Real example
Google DeepMind RT-X � one model trained on 22 robot embodiments at once.

World Models

"Practice in a video game first."
  • ›AI-built physics simulators of the real world.
  • ›1,000+ virtual robots rehearse in parallel.
  • ›Fail a billion times in sim � no broken motors.
  • ›The "Sim2Real bridge" transfers policies to physical hardware.
Real example
NVIDIA Cosmos � trained on 20M+ hours of real-world video to predict physics.
PART FOUR

Software-Defined Materiality

Investigating how physical production, fabrication, and structural engineering are moving from hardware limits to software loops.

Adaptive Assembly Lines

From welded-steel certainty to a factory that re-routes itself in software.

Legacy

Rigid Physical Setup

Traditional factories demand structured hardware configurations. Any change in part placement or geometry requires months of retooling downtime and manual adjustments.

  • • Months of assembly-line retooling downtime.
  • • Single-purpose hardware restricted to identical parts.
Modern

Software-Defined Kinematics

Modern facilities deploy software-managed movement loops. Multimodal platforms assess coordinates in real-time and re-route paths instantly without physical plant redesign.

  • • Zero-downtime line adjustments via software push.
  • • Dynamic paths handle varied geometries and SKUs.

Why Humans Stay in the Loop

"Full autonomy" is a marketing slogan. Reality needs a human anchor.

Why Pure Autonomy Fails

  • ›Physical mistakes have real liability � not just hallucinations.
  • ›Weather, spills, damage = infinite edge cases.
  • ›No simulation can pre-test every scenario.
Example · Autonomous Driving Drift
1. See
Pedestrian partially hidden behind a parked truck.
2. Mis-label
Labels as "traffic cone" at 51% � no flag, no human review.
3. Crash
Vehicle proceeds. Liability fully on the manufacturer.

A human steward would have paused at 51% confidence. Without HITL, perception errors compound into mechanical failures � in cars, in surgery, in cobot cells.

The Human Anchor

  • 1
    Tacit Knowledge
    How tight a bolt should feel. Wood grain. Can only be shown.
  • 2
    Edge-Case Failsafe
    Low confidence → flag a human steward.
  • 3
    Liability Anchor
    Insurance & law require a named human accountable.
