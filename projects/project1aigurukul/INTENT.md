## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document captures **what** AI-Gurukul 2.0 intends to accomplish and **why** — at a level sufficient for independent review by pedagogy experts, AI/domain experts, education-sector leaders, and financiers.

It deliberately excludes *how* (implementation methods), *when* (timelines), and *where* (sites and geographies). Those belong to downstream planning documents. Reviewers are asked to evaluate the **intent** — its validity, coherence, and ambition — and to flag where the stated goals are unsupported, unmeasurable, or misaligned.

---

## 2. The Intent (In One Paragraph)

India's ~9.5 million school and college teachers have had no meaningful preparation for the AI transformation of their profession. AI-Gurukul 2.0 intends to build a center that turns educators from anxious bystanders into confident, authoritative users and designers of AI in education — through mastery-based, project-driven, peer-evaluated learning whose every output is socially useful work. The trained educator is the unit of change; every certified teacher multiplies the impact to hundreds of students per year, compounding across their career.

---

## 3. Why This Matters (Problem Statement)

- **The technology arrived before the training.** Generative AI is already inside classrooms; educators were never equipped to use it well, so they either avoid it or use it superficially.
- **Existing teacher training does not work for this challenge.** Lecture-based, certificate-oriented, one-time events produce minimal lasting change; passive formats cannot teach an active skill.
- **Institutions cannot hire their way out.** AI-skilled faculty are priced out of academia by industry salaries; capability must be *grown internally*.
- **The cost of inaction compounds.** Every year of unprepared teachers produces cohorts of students entering an AI-first workforce without AI readiness.

**Root belief:** the gap is not information — it is a *learning environment* built for mastery, practice, and honest critique.

---

## 4. Vision & Mission

**Vision:** A teaching workforce that leads the AI age rather than fears it — beginning in India, with a model the world can reuse.

**Mission:** To train educators on AI through a mastery-gated, project-based, peer-evaluated program in which every learning output is socially useful work, verified by expert examination.

## 5. Guiding Principles (Non-Negotiables)

These constrain all downstream design decisions. Any future proposal that violates them is out of scope, regardless of convenience.

| # | Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Learning by doing, never by lecture** | No level may be completed through attendance, listening, or examination alone. Demonstrated applied capability is the only currency. |
| P2 | **Mastery before progression** | No learner advances to a higher level without demonstrating mastery of the current one. Sequencing is strict; there are no shortcuts, lateral entries, or honorific exemptions. |
| P3 | **Peer evaluation is sacred — and policed** | Peers evaluate each other's work as a core learning act. An independent expert body continuously audits evaluation *quality* so that peer review builds critical thinking and never decays into mutual accommodation. |
| P4 | **Every project must be socially useful** | No toy exercises. Every project solves a real problem for a real classroom, institution, community, or the open-source commons (Gandhian SUPW principle). |
| P5 | **The whole person** | The program develops judgment, ethics, and craft alongside technical skill — not technical skill alone. |
| P6 | **Openness at the frontier** | The program's advanced work is conducted in the open (open-source, public defenses) so that the commons, not just the individual, benefits. |
| P7 | **Educators first, sustainability always** | The primary beneficiaries are educators and their students; the enterprise must be financially self-sustaining without compromising P1–P6. |

## 6. High-Level Requirements (What Must Be True)

Each requirement is stated as an outcome or capability, not a method. Methods are open to review and revision; these requirements are the contract.

### 6.1 Program Architecture

- **R1.** The program shall define a small number of clearly distinct skill levels, ordered from foundational AI literacy to the ability to train and mentor other educators.
- **R2.** Progression between levels shall be strictly sequential and gated solely by demonstrated mastery, verified by an independent expert examination (viva).
- **R3.** The foundational levels shall be short, immersive, residential intensives (on the order of one week each), designed for working teachers and faculty.
- **R4.** The advanced levels shall be virtual and structured as collaborative open-source projects with public deliverables.
- **R5.** The highest level shall require demonstrated capability to develop *other* educators — making trainees-into-trainers the program's own faculty pipeline.

### 6.2 Learning Experience

- **R6.** Every level shall require each learner to complete at least one substantive project deployed against a real problem — not a simulation or classroom exercise.
- **R7.** Learning paths shall be personalized per learner (by discipline, prior knowledge, and role) rather than one-size-fits-all, using AI tutoring as the personalization mechanism.
- **R8.** Peer evaluation shall be a formal, rubric-structured component of every level, and the quality of a learner's *evaluations of others* shall itself be examined at viva.
- **R9.** The residential experience shall embody whole-person education — learning embedded in studios, workshops, nature, craft, and community life, not corridors and lecture halls.

### 6.3 Integrity & Quality Assurance

- **R10.** An independent **Expert Committee** (senior educators, AI specialists, practitioners) shall conduct the viva at every level and shall have authority to fail, require rework, or mandate re-evaluation — independent of program management.
- **R11.** The Expert Committee shall maintain an evaluation-calibration system that scores evaluators (peer and expert) against each other over time, so drift toward leniency is detected and corrected.
- **R12.** The program shall produce auditable evidence of mastery for every certified educator: project artifacts, evaluation history, and viva records.

### 6.4 Curriculum Content (What Must Be Taught)

- **R13.** Foundational levels shall cover: how modern AI works at a usable level of depth; effective use of AI for core teaching tasks; bias, ethics, privacy, and student safeguarding.
- **R14.** Practitioner levels shall cover: designing AI-native pedagogy, assessments, and personalized learning paths; using learner data to iterate on real teaching.
- **R15.** Content shall be adapted to learner segments: school teachers, college/university faculty, and institutional leaders.

### 6.5 Reach & Impact

- **R16.** The program shall be designed to scale from a single center to multiple geographies and to a fully virtual global track, without diluting R2, R3, P2, or P3.
- **R17.** Impact shall be measured by outcomes in real classrooms — deployment of AI-enabled practice by certified educators and effects on their students — not by certificates issued or seats filled.
- **R18.** Aspirational scale: tens of thousands of educators trained within five years of full operations, each multiplying impact to hundreds of students annually. (Targets to be validated by reviewers — see §9.)

### 6.6 Sustainability

- **R19.** The enterprise shall achieve financial sustainability through a mixed model (institutional partnerships, public/CSR programs, corporate training, certification, research) — without tuition dependence from individual teachers, and without compromising P1–P6.
- **R20.** The program shall generate and publish evidence about what works in AI-enabled educator training, contributing to the global commons (aligned with P6).

## 8. Scope Boundaries

**In scope:** the intent, principles, requirements, and success measures above; educator segments named in R15; the certification system; impact measurement.

**Out of scope (for this document):** implementation plans, timelines, budgets, site selection, technology stacks, staffing plans, legal structure, partnership agreements, marketing. These will be reviewed separately against this intent.

## 9. Open Questions for Reviewers

1. **Measurement (R17, R18):** Are the proposed outcome measures (classroom deployment, student effects) the right ones — and are they measurable at reasonable cost?
2. **Scale vs. integrity (R16 vs. P3):** Can the Expert Committee and calibration system (R10–R11) genuinely scale to thousands of learners per year without dilution? What is the breaking point?
3. **One-week intensives (R3):** Is one week per foundational level sufficient for lasting mastery, given that Level 2 explicitly requires a 2–3 week classroom deployment — or must the program formally span that deployment period?
4. **Viva audit of peer evaluation (R8, P3):** Is examining the *quality of critiques* a sound anti-gaming mechanism? How might it be gamed in turn?
5. **Sustainability (R19):** Is the mixed-revenue model credible in the Indian education funding landscape, and does the corporate-training cross-subsidy create mission drift risk?
6. **Open-source requirement (R4, P6):** Does mandating public/open work exclude educators whose institutional contexts (confidentiality, IP policies) prevent it?
7. **AI tutoring dependence (R7):** What happens to personalization quality when the underlying AI tools change or degrade? What is the fallback?

## 10. How to Review This Document

**For pedagogy & assessment experts:** focus on §5 (principles), R2, R3, R6–R9, R10–R12, and questions 2–4. Challenge whether mastery, peer evaluation, and viva mechanisms are educationally sound and game-resistant.

**For AI/domain experts:** focus on R7, R13–R14, and questions 6–7. Challenge whether the curriculum stays current and robust as AI capabilities shift.

**For education-sector leaders & pilot institutions:** focus on R15–R18 and questions 1, 3. Challenge realism of classroom deployment claims and institutional adoption.

**For financiers:** focus on R16, R18–R20 and questions 2, 5. Challenge the sustainability model, scale economics, and the integrity costs of growth.
