# Intent: AI-Gurukul 2.0 — Center of Excellence for Teacher Training

> **Source**: Derived from `INTENT.md` (v1.0) and `AI-Gurukul_2.0_Concept_Note.md` (v2.0).
> **Status**: Ready for review by pedagogy experts, AI/domain experts, education-sector leaders, and financiers.

---

## 1. Problem / Opportunity Statement

India's ~9.5 million school and college teachers have had no meaningful preparation for the AI transformation of their profession. Generative AI is already inside classrooms; educators were never equipped to use it well, so they either avoid it or use it superficially. Existing teacher-training models (lecture-based, certificate-chasing, one-time workshops) produce minimal lasting change — most workshop knowledge fades within 30 days without deliberate practice.

Meanwhile, institutions cannot hire their way out: AI-skilled faculty are priced out of academia by industry salaries (3–5× premium). The gap is not information — it is a *learning environment* built for mastery, practice, and honest critique.

**Root belief:** AI-Gurukul 2.0 builds that environment — a center where educators transform from anxious bystanders to confident, authoritative users and designers of AI in education.

## 3. Vision & Mission

**Vision:** A teaching workforce that leads the AI age rather than fears it — beginning in India, with a model the world can reuse.

**Mission:** To train educators on AI through a mastery-gated, project-based, peer-evaluated program in which every learning output is socially useful work, verified by expert examination.

---

## 4. The Program: Four Skill Levels

Progression is strictly sequential. Each level ends with a **Viva** before the next unlocks.

| Level | Name | Format | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🌱 **Level 1** | AI Literate | Residential intensive | 1 week on campus |
| 🌿 **Level 2** | AI Practitioner | Residential intensive | 1 week on campus (+2–3 week classroom deployment) |
| 🌳 **Level 3** | AI Innovator | Virtual, open-source collaborative | Ongoing cohort |
| 🏆 **Level 4** | AI Mentor | Virtual, open-source | Train the trainers |

**Viva Structure (all levels):** 45-minute Expert Committee defense of (a) the project, (b) the learner's *quality of peer evaluations given to others* — turning peer review into a discipline of critical thinking, not a courtesy.

---

## 5. Guiding Principles (Non-Negotiables)

| # | Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Learning by doing, never by lecture** | No level completed through attendance or examination alone. Demonstrated applied capability is the only currency. |
| P2 | **Mastery before progression** | No shortcuts, lateral entries, or honorific exemptions. Sequencing is strict. |
| P3 | **Peer evaluation is sacred — and policed** | Peer evaluation quality is itself examined; drift toward leniency is detected and corrected by the Expert Committee. |
| P4 | **Every project must be socially useful** | No toy exercises. Real problems, real classrooms, real communities (Gandhian SUPW). |
| P5 | **The whole person** | Judgment, ethics, and craft alongside technical skill. |
| P6 | **Openness at the frontier** | Advanced work is open-source and publicly defended — for the commons, not just the individual. |
| P7 | **Educators first, sustainability always** | Primary beneficiaries are educators and their students; enterprise must be financially self-sustaining without compromising P1–P6. |

## 6. High-Level Requirements (What Must Be True)

### 6.1 Program Architecture
- **R1.** A small number of clearly distinct, ordered skill levels from foundational AI literacy to training-the-trainer.
- **R2.** Progression strictly sequential; gated solely by demonstrated mastery, verified by independent Expert Committee viva.
- **R3.** Foundational levels: short, immersive, residential intensives (~1 week each) designed for working teachers.
- **R4.** Advanced levels: virtual, structured as collaborative open-source projects with public deliverables.
- **R5.** Highest level: trainees-into-trainers become the program's own faculty pipeline.

### 6.2 Learning Experience
- **R6.** Every level: at least one substantive project deployed against a real problem (not a simulation).
- **R7.** Learning paths personalized per learner (by discipline, prior knowledge, role) via AI tutoring.
- **R8.** Peer evaluation: formal, rubric-structured; quality of evaluations examined at viva.
- **R9.** Residential experience: studios, workshops, nature, craft, community life — not corridors and lecture halls.

### 6.3 Integrity & Quality Assurance
- **R10.** Independent Expert Committee conducts viva at every level; authority to fail or mandate rework.
- **R11.** Evaluation-calibration system: scores evaluators against each other over time; drift toward leniency detected and corrected.
- **R12.** Auditable evidence of mastery: project artifacts, evaluation history, viva records.

### 6.4 Curriculum Content
- **R13.** Foundational: how AI works; AI for teaching tasks; bias, ethics, privacy, student safeguarding.
- **R14.** Practitioner: AI-native pedagogy design; learner analytics; real classroom iteration.
- **R15.** Content adapted to: school teachers, college/university faculty, institutional leaders.

### 6.5 Reach & Impact
- **R16.** Designed to scale: single center → multiple geographies → virtual global track, without diluting integrity.
- **R17.** Impact measured by: classroom deployment rates, student outcomes — not certificates issued.
- **R18.** Aspirational: tens of thousands of educators trained within 5 years of full operations.

### 6.6 Sustainability
- **R19.** Mixed-revenue model: institutional partnerships, government/CSR, corporate training, certification, research — without tuition dependence.
- **R20.** Publish evidence of what works in AI-enabled educator training — contributing to the global commons.

## 8. Open Questions for Reviewers

1. **Measurement (R17, R18):** Are the proposed outcome measures (classroom deployment, student effects) the right ones — and are they measurable at reasonable cost?
2. **Scale vs. integrity (R16 vs. P3):** Can the Expert Committee and calibration system genuinely scale to thousands of learners per year without dilution? What is the breaking point?
3. **One-week intensives (R3):** Is one week per foundational level sufficient for lasting mastery, given Level 2's 2–3 week classroom deployment requirement?
4. **Viva audit of peer evaluation (R8, P3):** Is examining the quality of critiques a sound anti-gaming mechanism? How might it be gamed in turn?
5. **Sustainability (R19):** Is the mixed-revenue model credible in the Indian education funding landscape? Does corporate-training cross-subsidy create mission drift risk?
6. **Open-source requirement (R4, P6):** Does mandating public/open work exclude educators whose institutional contexts (confidentiality, IP policies) prevent it?
7. **AI tutoring dependence (R7):** What happens to personalization quality when the underlying AI tools change or degrade? What is the fallback?
