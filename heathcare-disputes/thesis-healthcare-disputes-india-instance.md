## B. Phase 1 draft: intent (`01_intent.md`)

### Problem statement (draft)

Healthcare disputes in India (negligence, deficiency in service, consent failures, billing, professional misconduct, hospital liability) can be pursued in several parallel forums: consumer commissions, civil courts, criminal courts, writ jurisdiction, medical councils and, increasingly, ADR. The standards are largely **judge-made** rather than codified, the forums overlap, and outcomes can diverge. *Evidence status: plausible; to be verified in Phase 2 against the authorities.*

### Working thesis statement (provisional, for the human to rewrite)

> India's regime for resolving healthcare disputes is fragmented across overlapping forums and rests largely on judicial standards; a coherent, accessible regime requires codified standards of liability, clearer forum allocation and structured ADR pathways.

This is a hypothesis to test in Phase 4, not a conclusion. If the analysis does not support it, the thesis statement changes (Playbook §4.6).

### Research questions (draft)

| # | Research question | Chapters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| RQ1 | What is the structure of the legal regime governing healthcare disputes in India, across constitutional, statutory, regulatory, civil, consumer, criminal and ADR routes? | 3, 4 |
| RQ2 | How have Indian courts developed the standards for medical negligence, informed consent, hospital and vicarious liability, burden and standard of proof, expert evidence and compensation? | 5 |
| RQ3 | How coherent is the interaction between these forums: where do they overlap, conflict or leave gaps, and with what consequences for patients, doctors and institutions? | 5, 7 |
| RQ4 | To what extent can mediation, conciliation, arbitration and Lok Adalat mechanisms be used for healthcare disputes, and what legal obstacles exist? | 4, 7 |
| RQ5 | What can India learn from comparator jurisdictions, and what reforms should it adopt? | 6, 8 |

### Scope

**In scope:** liability and dispute-resolution law for private and public healthcare providers; patient and provider rights relevant to disputes (consent, records, confidentiality as they bear on disputes); the forum structure; ADR; comparative lessons; reform proposals.

**Out of scope (default, human to confirm):** clinical standards of care as medical science; health insurance contract disputes except where they affect forum choice; pharmaceutical and device product liability; medical-research ethics regulation; empirical performance statistics of forums.

### Contribution (draft)

- **Doctrinal:** a systematic map of the Indian forums and the standards each applies, with conflicts identified.
- **Theoretical:** a framework linking the right to health and access to justice to dispute-resolution design.
- **Practical:** drafted reform proposals and a model ADR pathway for healthcare disputes.

## D. Starting-lead list for Phase 2 (ALL UNVERIFIED)

> **These are memory-based leads to search for, not authorities.** None may be cited in the thesis until the verification agent opens each in an official or recognised source and records it in `authorities_register.md` with pinpoints and a currency check (Doctrine D3, D4, D5). Names, citations and descriptions below may be wrong. A mismatch found during verification is expected and is not an error in itself; a wrong citation that reaches the thesis is a Critical issue.

**Constitutional and statutory instruments (verify text, amendment history and current status):**
- Constitution of India: Articles 21, 39, 41, 47 and the relevant Seventh Schedule entries.
- Consumer Protection Act 2019 and rules made under it (including mediation rules).
- Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act 2010 and State equivalents.
- National Medical Commission Act 2019, and the professional-conduct regulations in force.
- Indian Medical Council Act 1956 (historical) and the 2002 conduct regulations (historical).
- Criminal law on causing death by negligence: the former IPC provision and its BNS counterpart, with procedural counterparts in the BNSS.
- Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996; Mediation Act 2023; Legal Services Authorities Act 1987.
- Law of contract and tort (for the civil route); Evidence law on expert opinion.
- Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; telemedicine guidelines; Mental Healthcare Act 2017; related health statutes as scope requires.

**Case-law leads (search by name and topic; verify everything):**
- Indian Medical Association v V.P. Shantha (medical services and consumer law).
- Jacob Mathew v State of Punjab (criminal negligence standard for doctors).
- Samira Kohli v Dr Prabha Manchanda (informed consent).
- Martin F. D'Souza v Mohd. Ishfaq (procedural guidance on notice to doctors in consumer complaints, and its later treatment).
- Kusum Sharma v Batra Hospital and Medical Research Centre (negligence principles).
- Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity v State of West Bengal and Parmanand Katara v Union of India (emergency care and the right to life).
- Common Cause v Union of India (end-of-life, advance directives).
- UK comparator leads: the Bolam and Bolitho line, and Montgomery on consent.
- Later Supreme Court and NCDRC decisions on hospital liability, burden of proof, expert evidence and compensation: **to be found by search, not from memory.**

**Secondary-source leads:** Law Commission of India reports on medical practice and consumer law; parliamentary committee reports on health and consumer affairs; leading Indian and comparative treatises and journal articles on medical negligence and healthcare ADR: **to be identified by search and verified.**
