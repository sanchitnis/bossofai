# Habit-Formation Research

What the evidence supports for a product that asks people to practise daily. Verified items cite a source; **unverified** items are candidates to check before use.

## Verified (from a research summary)
- **Lally et al. (2010)** followed 96 undergraduates (82 completed) over 84 days. Reaching a habit-level automaticity took on average about **66 days**, with a very wide range (**18 to a predicted 254 days**). Roughly half had not reached a habit-level score by day 84. A **single missed day made little difference**, but many missed repetitions lowered the level eventually reached. Automaticity was **self-reported** and the sample was students, so generalisation is limited. *Source: [BPS Research Digest: How long to form a habit](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit), accessed 2026-10-16.*

### What this means for our design
- An **8-week window is short** for many learners to reach automaticity. Use 8 weeks for a first read on practice and baseline change, and a **12-week or longer** check for habit-automaticity claims.
- **Weekly consistency** targets, not unbroken daily chains: one missed day is not fatal.
- Expect large individual differences; avoid promising a fixed number of days.

## Candidates to verify before use (unverified)
| Technique | Idea | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Implementation intentions** (Gollwitzer) | "If situation X, then I will do Y" plans | Believed to have medium-to-large effects in meta-analyses (e.g. Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006); not verified in this session (a source was blocked by bot protection) |
| **Tiny Habits / Fogg Behaviour Model** (BJ Fogg) | Behaviour needs motivation, ability and a prompt; start tiny, celebrate | Described in popular and book sources only; research basis not verified |
| **Spaced repetition and retrieval practice** | Revisit material at growing intervals; recall instead of re-reading | Well known in cognitive psychology; sources to cite before use |
| **Self-determination theory** | Autonomy, competence and relatedness support motivation | To verify |
| **Mental contrasting with implementation intentions (WOOP)** | Imagine the wish, the obstacle, then plan | To verify |

None of these is used as a claim in learner-facing copy until a source is attached in `claims.md`.
