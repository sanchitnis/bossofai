# Atomic Habits: Behaviour Design for Daily Practice

A popular framework by James Clear (book: *Atomic Habits*, 2018) for building and breaking habits through small changes. This page is a **paraphrased summary with attribution**, not a reproduction of the book. *Secondary source for this summary: [MyLifeNote: 4 Laws of Behavior Change](https://blog.mylifenote.ai/4-laws-of-behavior-change-build-habits/), accessed 2026-10-16. Book details (author, year) are from general knowledge and not re-checked.*

> **Copyright note.** The book is copyrighted. Ideas and general methods are free to describe; the book's text, examples, diagrams and distinctive phrasing are not ours to copy. PROdiGYM writes its own wording, examples and exercises and credits Clear as the source of the framework.

## The framework (paraphrased)
A habit runs as a loop of **cue, craving, response and reward**. Each of four "laws" targets one step:
1. **Make the cue obvious:** decide exactly when and where you will act; arrange your surroundings so the trigger is hard to miss.
2. **Make it attractive:** pair the needed habit with something you enjoy; spend time with people who already do it; reframe it as an opportunity.
3. **Make it easy:** start with a tiny version; remove steps; prepare your space beforehand.
4. **Make it satisfying:** get an immediate sign of progress; track it visibly; avoid missing twice in a row; use a partner or commitment.

Related techniques in the same framework: **implementation intentions** (a specific time, place and action), **habit stacking** (attach a new habit to an existing routine), the **two-minute rule** (a starter version under two minutes), **environment design**, **habit tracking**, and **identity-based habits** (act as the kind of person you want to be). To break a habit, invert each law.

## Evidence status
- A practical, widely used framework, not itself a tested intervention. Some building blocks have research behind them (see [[habit_formation_research|habit-formation research]]); the framework as a whole should be treated as a hypothesis to test in our pilot.

## Application in PROdiGYM (proposed; Sanjay to confirm)
- **Verdict: use as the method layer for the MVP's "Taking Charge of Life" domain.** Every habit in the catalogue is practised through a short plan: cue, tiny version, environment tweak, tracking, and a reward or reflection.
- Our own "habit plan" template (in our wording): *When [cue], I will [tiny action], in [place]; I will make it easier by [change]; I will track it by [method]; I will check in with [partner]*.
- Identity prompts link to **Prakamya**: not only "who do I want to be?" but "what is worth wanting, and for whom?".
