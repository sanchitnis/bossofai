# 🧠 Minerva Project: Active Learning & Habits of Mind (HCAs)

Founded by Ben Nelson and Stephen Kosslyn (former Chair of Harvard Psychology), **The Minerva Project / Minerva University** represents a radical redesign of higher education. Minerva eliminates traditional passive lectures in favor of **Active Learning Seminars** built around cross-contextual **Habits of Mind and Foundational Concepts (HCAs)**.

## 🛠️ Key Mechanisms: Habits of Mind & Foundational Concepts (HCAs)

### What are HCAs?
- **Habit of Mind**: An cognitive skill that becomes automatic through practice (e.g., `#rightproblem` — framing the correct problem before proposing solutions).
- **Foundational Concept**: A specific fundamental principle across disciplines (e.g., `#correlation` vs `#causation`, `#heuristic`, `#bias`).

### Cross-Contextual Rubric Grading
Minerva professors evaluate student work not on static memorization, but on **how accurately students apply specific HCAs** in novel, unfamiliar contexts (e.g., applying `#game-theory` to urban transport or healthcare policy).

## 💡 Application in Project PRODIGY

Minerva's HCA framework directly informs **PRODIGY's HEITL Talent Pipeline**:
- **The 4 Cs Alignment**: Minerva's 4 Core Competencies directly map to PRODIGY's **4 Cs** (Critical Thinking, Communication, Collaboration, Creativity).
- **HCA Rubrics for AI Co-Working**: Evaluates how student leads prompt, audit, and refine AI outputs (e.g., `#prompt-rigor`, `#algorithmic-bias`, `#root-cause-deconstruction`).
- **HEITL Jury Assessment**: Expert jury evaluates Stage 3 probes based on HCA transferability across real-world enterprise and social environments.
