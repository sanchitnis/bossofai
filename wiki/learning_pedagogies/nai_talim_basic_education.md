# Nai Talim (Basic Education)

Mahatma Gandhi's educational philosophy: **knowledge and work are not separate**, and education should be lifelong, social and holistic, a moral as well as intellectual development. *Sources: [Wikipedia: Nai Talim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nai_Talim); [MK Gandhi Sevagram: Basic Education](https://www.mkgandhi.org/sevagram/basic_edu.php), both accessed 2026-10-16.*

## Key ideas (as described by these sources)
- **Learning through productive craft** (spinning, weaving, gardening, pottery and similar), taught so the learner understands the reasons behind each process; subjects such as mathematics are learned through the craft.
- **Self-reliance:** Gandhi argued that a school can support itself through productive work. Literacy is a means, not education itself.
- **Whole person:** physical, mental and moral development together.
- **History:** early experiments in South Africa; a national education conference at Wardha on 22 to 23 October 1937, followed by two model schools; later extended by Vinoba Bhave (teachers and students as fellow workers).
- **Criticism:** the national planning of the time had no place for self-supporting schools; the scheme did not survive the shift to centralised development planning (per the Wikipedia article's summary).

Not covered by the sources we read: instruction in the mother tongue, the "head, heart, hand" formulation, and the Zakir Husain committee. Add them only after checking a source.

## Application in PROdiGYM (proposed; Sanjay to confirm)
- **Verdict: use as a philosophical anchor, alongside Prakamya.** Both say learning should serve others.
- Concrete uses: practice that produces something **useful to another person or the community** (not only a score); a "learn by doing, then reflect" structure (it matches [[kolb_experiential_learning|Kolb]]); community-benefit prompts in challenges.
